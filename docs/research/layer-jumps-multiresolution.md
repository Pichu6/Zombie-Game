# Layer jumps: what multi-resolution simulation research and games say

**Date:** 2026-10-06. **Status:** research note, not a decision. **Feeds:** [layer jumps](../design/layer-jumps.md), [focus and compression](../design/focus-and-compression.md), [world simulation](../design/world-simulation.md), [pop continuity](../design/pop-continuity.md), [spec 27](../tier0/specs/27-detail-levels.md), [spec 28](../tier0/specs/28-pop-levels.md). **Evidence:** WebSearch result summaries only; WebFetch was blocked by the egress proxy for every academic and game-wiki host tried (tudelft, jasss, arxiv, mdpi, doi.org, aimsun docs, starcitizen.tools, debian mirror), so no paper was read in full. The failure-mode list and test patterns are our own synthesis; where a failure mode has a source it is cited, otherwise it is marked "our synthesis". **IDs:** LJ.

Asked: what do multi-resolution / multi-fidelity simulation research and real games say about aggregation and disaggregation, and what failure modes and test patterns should we plan for between our layers (Macro, City, Individual)? Context: [world-simulation §2 and §8](../design/world-simulation.md), [pop continuity](../design/pop-continuity.md), [spec 27](../tier0/specs/27-detail-levels.md), [spec 28](../tier0/specs/28-pop-levels.md), [detail-levels research](detail-levels.md).

## Failure modes

Numbers 1-4 and 6 are named in the MRM literature above; the rest are our synthesis from the same sources and from our own specs. Each has a symptom a player or designer would see and a test.

1. **Non-unique disaggregation** (aggregation erased detail). Symptom: a cold region of 412 walkers wakes with a different crowd (ages, wounds) each time you enter it. Test: wake the same saved aggregate with the same keys twice and across save/load; the promoted entities must be identical. Also check each promoted set re-aggregates to the stored aggregate (round trip).
2. **Round-trip drift** (aggregate, disaggregate, aggregate changes totals or loses detail). Symptom: walking into and out of a block repeatedly makes its food or population creep up or down. Test: N round trips with no simulated time between them must leave the ledger and a state hash unchanged; with time between them, equal to stepping.
3. **Chain disaggregation.** Symptom: waking one house wakes the street, then the district, and the frame freezes. Test: a budget assertion: waking any chunk promotes at most K entities per tick and never promotes outside the rule-defined ring; fuzz many wake orders and assert the hot set stays under the cap.
4. **Thrashing at the boundary.** Symptom: a pop at the edge of the hot ring flickers between record and body every few ticks, repeating arrival and departure events. Test: move a body back and forth across the ring edge by one cell and count level changes; the count must be bounded (hysteresis margin or minimum dwell time). Hysteresis as an MRM remedy was not found in my sources; it is our synthesis.
5. **Temporal inconsistency** (parts of the world at different times). Symptom: a cold house shows food spoiled for 18 h next to a hot house at hour 12, or a generator is heard but its fuel is not burning. Test: assert that every chunk's `caught_up_tick` equals now after a wake and that cross-border fields use the same tick on both sides.
6. **Loss of correlation and heterogeneity.** Symptom: aggregate says 40 of 120 laid off; promoted workers show 55 or 20 laid off, or all the laid-off are the skilled ones by accident. Test: aggregate event vs enumeration (the count of promoted pops reporting the event equals the aggregate count, for random subsets); compare distributions of attributes (age, skill) inside and outside the affected set to the pre-event ones.
7. **Aggregate and detail disagree in distribution** (the Bannerlord auto-resolve complaint). Symptom: a squad of veterans always dies off-screen but wins when played. Test: many seeded hot runs vs one jump (spec 27 A2), compared by mean and variance for the quantities that matter.
8. **Created or destroyed conserved things** (duplicated or vanished people, items, litres). Symptom: a stack of tins appears twice, or a survivor is missing from every layer. Test: ledger totals before and after every promotion, demotion and jump; stateful fuzzing of random wake/sleep sequences against a model ledger.
9. **Two sources of truth.** Symptom: Macro panel shows 412 walkers while the street has 409. Test: Macro counts are computed as sums over the lower layer (spec 28 §3); assert equality on random states rather than allowing a stored duplicate.
10. **Non-idempotent wake.** Symptom: waking twice (or save, load, wake) applies spoilage or arrivals twice. Test: `advance(from, to)` followed by `advance(to, to)` is a no-op; save and reload mid-wake gives the same hash.
11. **Order dependence of jumps** (waking early vs late gives different results). Symptom: the world differs depending on whether you visited the chunk yesterday. Test: wake at t1 then t2 vs directly at t2; hash equal for deterministic rules, equal in distribution for random ones. Requires draws keyed by entity, purpose and tick (counter-based RNG).
12. **Rules that cannot jump are jumped anyway** (fights, door pushes). Symptom: a fight resolves off-screen with a formula that a played fight would never produce. Test: every rule declares jumpable or hot-only; a test fails if a hot-only rule is called from `advance`.
13. **Border effects.** Symptom: a generator running in a hot chunk is silent in the cold house next door. Test: spec 27 A3, plus noise/light sampled across a border at each level pair.
14. **Boundary model mismatch** (the hybrid ABM-PDE and traffic lesson: the two models must be equivalent). Symptom: a visible jump in infection rate or traffic flow where the layers meet. Test: run the same scenario all-hot and with a cold half; compare flows across the boundary.
15. **Fragmentation of aggregate groups** (Victoria 3 pop splitting). Symptom: record counts grow without bound over a long Macro run and frame time rises. Test: a long run asserting record count stays under the budget, and rosters merge when equal to what the seed gives (spec 28 demotion rule).
16. **Wake-cost spikes.** Symptom: a frame hitch when entering a dense block. Test: benchmark the worst-case wake and assert promotion is spread over a bounded number of ticks.
17. **Rare-event loss at coarse step.** Symptom: a death or bite that would have happened in a hot hour never happens in a one-jump day, or happens with the wrong probability. Test: compare event-count distributions, including tails, of one jump vs stepped runs; our synthesis.
18. **Exploits of the seam** (economy). Symptom: dropping items in a chunk, leaving, and returning yields more (or sleeping a chunk to dodge decay). Test: scripted "leave and return" sequences must give the same ledger as staying; our synthesis, no game evidence found.
19. **Shortcut teleports** (CS1 and CS2 style). Symptom: a stuck pop vanishes and reappears at its destination, hiding a bug and changing travel time. Test: count teleports in long runs and fail above a threshold; each must be logged with a reason.

## Patterns that work

- **One model, several resolutions, not several models.** Aimsun shares one network and database for meso and micro; X4 runs the same ships at two fidelities; hybrid epidemic work shows the cost of non-equivalent models. Sources above.
- **Pseudo and partial disaggregation:** promote only what is needed, and compute the rest as numbers. Source: https://doi.org/10.3390/info16080635 (summary).
- **Transition zone or ring** where both representations coexist (traffic hybrids; our "hot plus one ring"). Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.04009.
- **Counter-based, keyed draws** so results do not depend on when or in what order things are computed. Source: https://www.thesalmons.org/john/random123/releases/latest/docs/.
- **Handoff by removal and re-creation with a ledger check** (ABM-PDE: remove agent, add density; add agent from surplus). Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.08430.
- **Single authority per entity** (Star Citizen server meshing). Source: https://api.star-citizen.wiki/comm-links/19471.
- **Make auto-resolve call the same rules as the detailed fight** (the Bannerlord mod's per-unit approach). Source: https://nexusmods.com/mountandblade2bannerlord/mods/3453.
- **Agreement in distribution plus exact equality for deterministic rules**, checked with metamorphic and round-trip tests. Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.11031, https://cis.upenn.edu/~plclub/blog/2023-12-07-round-trip-properties.

## How this applies to our three layers

- Our spec 27/28 design (one sim, ledger-checked promotion, keyed draws, Macro as sums, ranking-based choice of affected pops) already addresses failure modes 1, 6, 8, 9, 11; no source contradicted it.
- Highest-risk gaps to add tests for first: thrashing at the ring edge (4), idempotent wake (10), chain wake budget (3), and the leave-and-return exploit (18). Spec 27 has no hysteresis or dwell rule yet (our reading of the spec).
- Treat "jump vs stepped" (A2) as agreement in distribution including tails (17), not only means.
- Add a "hot-only" flag per rule (12) so Macro and City never resolve a fight the player could still watch.
- Add a boundary test per layer pair (13, 14): same scenario all-hot vs partly cold, compare flows across the border.
- Track a long-run record-count budget (15), since Paradox games report pop fragmentation as a late-game cost.
- Log every forced shortcut (teleport, despawn, auto-resolve) with a reason (19), and cap them in tests.
- Pop-level choices (lazy, record, embodied) fit the "partial disaggregation" pattern; the open budget numbers in pop-continuity §7 remain unmeasured.
