# Country formation: how states, borders, sizes and wealth follow geography, and how generators make political maps

**Date:** 2026-10-09. **Status:** research note, not a decision. **Feeds:** [0059](../decisions/0059-countries-from-water-land-and-resources-everything-alive-can-be-sick.md), [spec 52](../tier0/specs/52-land-water-capacity.md), [world systems](../design/world-systems.md), [macro layer](../design/macro-layer.md). **Evidence:** read in full or in the relevant sections: Alesina, Easterly and Matuszeski, "Artificial States" (NBER w12328 PDF); Fernández-Villaverde, Koyama, Lin and Sng, "The Fractured-Land Hypothesis" (NBER w27774 PDF, model and calibration sections); Faye, McArthur, Sachs and Snow 2004 (PDF, first sections); Turchin et al. 2013 (PNAS, PMC full text, read through a page summary); Azgaar's Fantasy Map Generator source, `modules/burgs-and-states.js` at tag v1.99 (read through a page summary). Everything else is from web-search summaries, abstracts and wiki pages (`reported`). The web-search budget ran out part way, so these were **not** found or not read: Lake and Jarrett on country sizes (no such paper turned up), the share of borders on mountains and watersheds, Nunn and Puga on ruggedness, Kitamura and Lagerlöf, Cioffi-Revilla's polity models, Cederman's GeoSim rules, Songs of Syx, Crusader Kings III history generation, Scott's *Against the Grain* beyond WNN-3, and the full Sachs and Warner and Gallup, Sachs and Mellinger papers. **IDs:** CF.

On 2026-10-09 decision 0059 asked that countries form "around water, land and resources", with natural borders, unequal sizes and wealth from what the land holds, and that starts be picked by geography. Spec 48 still forms countries by suitability, three sectors each, and income by suitability times luck. This note gathers what history, economics and other generators say, so spec 52 slice 4 can replace that. It does not repeat [WNN](world-numbers-nations.md) (state thresholds, Carneiro, Scott's grain, Turchin's 65 %, size power laws) or [WSF](world-systems-factions.md) (power over distance, state death, Seshat licence); it cites them.

## 1. How states formed: what the evidence says

**Where the first states stood.** The strongest pattern is not "rich land makes states" but "land that can be taxed and cannot be left". Carneiro's hemmed-in valleys (CF-1, WNN-2), Scott's grain (WNN-3) and the cereal test of Mayshar, Moav and Pascali (CF-5) agree: states rise on farmland that grows storable grain and is bordered by land nobody wants. Irrigation came after the state in most cases (CF-4), so a river is a reason for a core because of the grain and water it brings, not because canals need a bureaucracy (CF-3). Counter-cases exist (CF-2), which is why the history step should be a contest with chance, not a rule that always fires.

**Where states did not reach.** Low density and open land let people leave (Herbst, CF-11), and hills let them hide (Scott, CF-13). Both point to the same mechanic: power fades with distance and with terrain cost (WSF-16, WSF-30), and land beyond reach stays unruled or loosely held. Herbst's shared tribute zones and Zomia are the same thing at two scales.

**Why some regions unify and others fragment.** Two quantitative models answer this with few rules. Turchin et al. (CF-8, CF-9, WNN-4) need military technology spreading from the steppe and elevation as defence. Fernández-Villaverde et al. (CF-24..29) need only productivity and terrain: a single large productive core (north China) unifies its region, while scattered cores behind mountains and seas (Europe) stay divided. Their model is the closest thing in print to what spec 52 slice 4 needs, and its rules fit our grid (CF-44). Its known failure (CF-29) is that it over-unifies Africa and the Americas, where disease, low density and slow crop spread held states back; our density threshold (P3) and the land's own capacity should catch part of that.

**Size and number.** Alesina and Spolaore (CF-19) explain the count as a trade-off and predict more, smaller countries with trade and democracy. Earth's sizes are very unequal: ten countries hold about half the land (CF-35, CF-36), and sizes follow a power law with exponent near 1 (WNN-7). Europe went from about 500 states to about 30 (WSF-22). These give the targets for P6.

**Borders.** About a quarter of international borders follow rivers, nearly half in South America (CF-23). Straight borders mark land divided by outsiders or before settlement (CF-20, CF-22), and they go with poorer outcomes (CF-21). A generated world without colonial drawing should have mostly squiggly borders on rivers, ridges and watersheds, with straight lines only where empty land was split late (P5). Shares for mountain and watershed borders were not found.

**Wealth.** Geography acts on income through transport, disease and farm yields (CF-16, CF-15), and institutions also matter (CF-14); the debate is open, so wealth should be endowment plus history, as 0059 says. Being landlocked costs about 40 % of income in developing countries (CF-30), mostly through the border and the transit neighbour, not distance (CF-31, CF-32, CF-33). Islands pay for small size and remoteness (CF-34) but gain the sea's protection (CF-26). Resource wealth helps growth as a stock; the "curse" sits in export dependence and weak institutions and is disputed (CF-17, CF-18).

## 3. What this means for us (Proposal)

All of this section is `estimate`, tied to the findings by ID.

**Pipeline for spec 52 slice 4** (runs once at world creation, pure function of grid, fields, land, deposits and seed):

1. **Core values** y per cell (CF-45): food capacity x cereal share x river and coast bonus x circumscription bonus.
2. **Polities at the start** (CF-47): each cell with enough people for a state (WNN-32) is a polity; the rest is unruled.
3. **History** (CF-47, CF-25..27): for T periods, each border cell may fight with probability α·y; the opponent is drawn among neighbours (land, or sea within 330 km at weight 0.1); the winner by total y, damped by distance from its capital (WSF-30); stalemate by terrain (Θ·x); secession of border cells by β; split polities become two. Every annexation and secession is recorded with its reason (world systems rule 2) and becomes the country's chronicle.
4. **Fragmentation** (CF-48): one slider sets β, Θ and T together, middle calibrated against Earth's count and concentration.
5. **Present-day fill** (CF-49): leftover land joins its cheapest neighbour by P2 cost, unless too far, then it becomes a small state.
6. **Capitals and sectors**: the capital is the country's highest-y cell held longest; sectors (spec 48 rule 3) are cut inside each country, so the fixed three sectors per country goes.
7. **Wealth** (CF-51, CF-52): endowment x access x history, then income groups by WPP shares as now.
8. **Card and starts** (CF-53, CF-54).

**Numbers to calibrate first**: α (keep 1 / max y), Θ so the 90th-percentile rugged cell stalemates 2 in 3 (CF-26), β around 1e-5 per border cell scaled to our cell size, T between 100 and 500 periods; check CF-50's targets on the Earth-like default and on two other seeds.

**Why this order**: it puts the reasons first (land, water, grain, terrain) and lets sizes come out of a contest, which is what 0059 asks ("sizes come out unequal, as on Earth"); the flood alone gives near-equal countries (CF-43).
