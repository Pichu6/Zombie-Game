# Story building blocks: how games and story theory build variety from parts

**Date:** 2026-10-09. **Status:** research note, not a decision. **Feeds:** [inhabitants](../design/inhabitants.md) (bonds, background), [content tools](../design/content-tools.md) (culture packs, lifepath), [spec 24](../tier0/specs/24-pop-ai.md), [spec 25](../tier0/specs/25-chronicle.md), and the design drafts [story-blocks](../design/story-blocks.md), [story-explorer](../design/story-explorer.md) and [cases-and-living-lore](../design/cases-and-living-lore.md). **Evidence:**
- **Search summaries only:** Wikipedia and course pages on Propp and Polti; the EPJ Data Science abstract (Reagan et al. 2016); Emily Short's blog posts of 2016 and 2019; Emily Short's report of Elan Ruskin's GDC 2012 talk (the talk itself and its slides were not read); the Wildermyth wiki, a developer devlog and reviews; the Tracery paper's abstract.
- **Read in our docs:** inhabitants, content tools.

**IDs:** SB.


The user asked whether pre-written "building blocks" for different kinds of things and contexts could be put together in different forms. The aim is a variety of stories of every kind (love, thrill, sadness, action, adventure), not only crime.

## Findings

| ID | Finding | Confidence | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SB-1 | Propp, *Morphology of the Folktale* (1928; English 1958): from about 100 Russian wonder tales, 31 **functions** (a character's act, by what it does for the plot) in a fixed order, though no tale uses all of them. Functions stay the same whoever performs them; the characters and items are the variables. The cast falls into 7 **spheres of action** (villain, hero, helper, donor and others) | reported | faculty.georgetown.edu Propp page; newworldencyclopedia.org Vladimir Propp |
| SB-2 | Polti, *The Thirty-Six Dramatic Situations* (1895; English 1916), a catalogue of every dramatic situation. No. 23 is "Necessity of sacrificing loved ones" and no. 35 "Recovery of a lost one" | reported | en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Thirty-Six_Dramatic_Situations |
| SB-3 | Reagan et al., "The emotional arcs of stories are dominated by six basic shapes" (EPJ Data Science, 2016). In 1,327 Project Gutenberg stories, the arcs cluster into six shapes: rags to riches (rise), tragedy (fall), man in a hole (fall then rise), Icarus (rise then fall), Cinderella (rise, fall, rise) and Oedipus (fall, rise, fall) | reported | epjds.epj.org 2016 article 93; arXiv 1606.07772 |
| SB-4 | Emily Short (2019): a **storylet** is a piece of content, with prerequisites for when it can appear and effects on the world state after it plays; storylets are "simple, atomic, robust, and recombinable". The term comes from Failbetter's *Echo Bazaar* (later *Fallen London*) | reported | emshort.blog/2019/11/29/storylets-you-want-them |
| SB-5 | Short (2016): in **quality-based** narrative the player chooses among the pieces whose prerequisites hold; in **salience-based** narrative the system picks the piece that best fits the moment. Alexis Kennedy, who coined "quality-based", later proposed "resource-based narrative" | reported | emshort.blog 2016-04-12 "Beyond branching" |
| SB-6 | Valve's dynamic dialogue (Elan Ruskin, GDC 2012, used in *Left 4 Dead*): hundreds of world facts are matched against thousands of lines. The most specific applicable rule wins and broader rules are fallbacks, cascading from special cases to general ones, so writers add special lines without rewriting the general ones | reported | emshort.blog 2012-03-16; gdcvault.com/play/1015317 (abstract) |
| SB-7 | Wildermyth casts events "like a play": each event names roles (targets) with criteria (traits, hooks, relationships), and happens only if the party can fill them. Trait strength changes the odds, and outcomes include changed relationships. Each chapter has a written opening and ending; everything in between is procedural | reported | wildermyth.com/wiki Story Inputs and Outputs, Comic Editor Reference; turnbasedlovers.com developer interview |
| SB-8 | Wildermyth's critics: after several campaigns players see the patterns, and there are "not enough events to go around", so the same ones repeat with names swapped | reported | rpgsite.net review; superjumpmagazine.com |
| SB-9 | Tracery (Compton, Kybartas and Mateas, ICIDS 2015): a generative-text grammar for writers who are not programmers, built for data portability, modular design and additive authoring | reported | graphics.tudelft.nl Publications 2015/CKM15 |
| SB-10 | Max Kreminski and Noah Wardrip-Fruin mapped the storylet design space at ICIDS 2018, covering tools such as StoryNexus and *Reigns* | reported | emshort.blog storylets tag; docs.yarnspinner.dev storylets primer |
| SB-11 | Our bonds design: one person's view of another has familiarity, affinity, trust, respect, fear, resentment, dependence and optional attraction; bonds are asymmetric. Healing raises trust or debt, not affection, and "giving the optimal gift does not buy friendship" | sourced | [inhabitants](../design/inhabitants.md), Bonds |
| SB-12 | Our content is already data blocks: culture packs (names, customs) and lifepath rules in data files, written to be extended | sourced | [content tools](../design/content-tools.md) §3, §4.1 |
| SB-13 | **Proposal:** blocks write intentions and words, never outcomes. Storylets and Wildermyth events carry effects (SB-4, SB-7); ours change the world only through actions pops choose under the normal rules. This keeps "found, never written" and "watching changes nothing" (0061) | derived | SB-4, SB-7; [story-explorer](../design/story-explorer.md) §2 |
| SB-14 | **Proposal:** casting as in Wildermyth for wants and shapes (roles with criteria filled from real pops); the most-specific-wins rule from Valve for the lines that tell what happened | derived | SB-6, SB-7 |
| SB-15 | **Proposal:** tone is computed. A pop's fortune over time (safety, health, bonds, hope) is matched to Reagan's six shapes, so "sad" or "uplifting" is measured from the simulation, not written in | estimate | SB-3 |
| SB-16 | Zombie fiction maps straight onto Polti's catalogue, and our simulation produces those situations by itself: no. 23 is putting down a bitten husband (spec 21), no. 35 is finding a sister alive (derived ties) | derived | SB-2; spec 21; [story-explorer](../design/story-explorer.md) §4 |
| SB-17 | **Proposal:** variety should come from outcomes, which the simulation supplies without end, more than from the number of blocks. This answers Wildermyth's repetition (SB-8). Repetition in the telling is measured like the oatmeal test (LB-41) | estimate | SB-8; [LB-41](library-of-babel-and-sacred-geometry.md) |
