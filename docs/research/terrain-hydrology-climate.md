# Terrain, hydrology, climate classes and soils: the next physical generator steps

**Date:** 2026-10-09. **Status:** research note, not a decision. **Feeds:** [spec 31](../tier0/specs/31-terrain-climate.md), [spec 52](../tier0/specs/52-land-water-capacity.md), [zoom continuum](../design/zoom-continuum.md), [world systems](../design/world-systems.md). **Evidence:** web-search summaries, plus these primary pages read: Beck et al. 2018 (PMC full text, the Köppen table), Lugo et al. 1999 (the Holdridge PET ratio), Nick McDonald's procedural hydrology post, Red Blob's river-growing page, the WorldEngine biome docs, the Schott 2023 code repository; Wikipedia read for river discharge, endorheic basins, Strahler numbers and drainage density. Not read: Cordonnier 2016 (PDF too large, HAL blocked), Tzathas 2024 (403), Braun and Willett 2013, Fekete 2002, FAO GAEZ tables, Thornthwaite 1948. The search budget ran out before stream power K values, lake evaporation rates and the Ganges-Brahmaputra totals could be checked; those rows say so. **IDs:** HY.

Spec 52 slice 1 gives runoff, drainage, lakes and rivers, but erosion, inland basins, monthly climate, sourced soil tables and rivers below the 88,500 km² cell are missing. This note collects the numbers and methods for those steps. It builds on [WG](world-generation-prior-art.md) (WG-5 to WG-8, WG-13, WG-16 to WG-20) and [RV](rivers-and-runoff.md) (RV-1 to RV-9) and does not repeat them.

## Erosion: what fits a 300 km grid

The stream power family is the right physics for the planet grid; particle and grid hydraulic erosion are the right tools for metres (HY-13). On our grid, a time-stepped FastScape run (HY-1, HY-2) is cheap, but a steady-state solve is cheaper and simpler: with n = 1, each cell's height is its downstream height plus the cell spacing times U / (K A^m) (HY-5). It runs in the Priority-Flood order we already have, has no time step, and gives valleys where catchments are large and ridges where they are small. Uplift comes from spec 31's boundary sources (collision, subduction, arc), so mountains keep their place and gain drainage-shaped relief. The analytical work of Tzathas et al. (HY-6) and Schott's uplift-domain authoring (HY-7) are the published versions of the same idea. A talus cap (HY-9) keeps slopes believable after the solve.

Calibration: the K/U ratio and m set how high the steady relief stands. Spec 31's hypsometry test (about 11 % of land above 1,000 m, mean ocean depth about 3,700 m) is the target; Cordonnier's 0-3 mm per year (HY-4) is a scale for U, but the paper's K was not read.

## Climate classes

Köppen needs the coldest and warmest months, months above 10 °C, and rain by season (HY-34). Our fields give the temperature part through HY-32; the rain part needs HY-53. Holdridge needs only biotemperature (from the same months) and annual rain (HY-37), and its PET is a sourced alternative to our RV-5 (HY-38). Beck's 1 km map (HY-35) gives the target shares of each class on Earth: count the GeoTIFF's pixels by area, since the shares were not in the text read.

## Soils

Sourced inputs for fertility: soil order shares (HY-40) and the drivers in HY-41: parent material, weathering age, climate. Our generator already knows the setting from spec 52 slice 3 (craton, arc, rift, basin, laterite), so fertility can come from setting plus climate instead of biome alone: alluvium and young volcanic arcs rich, grassland Mollisols rich, old tropical cratons poor (Oxisols), arid soils limited by water and salt (HY-45).

## What this means for us (Proposal)

In order, each step re-runnable and tested before the next:

1. **Endorheic basins** (HY-54): closes spec 52 Open 1 and is the cheapest step.
2. **Steady-state stream power** (HY-55): one pass on the flood order, calibrated to spec 31's hypsometry.
3. **Monthly climate and Köppen** (HY-56): months from mean and range plus a rain seasonality model.
4. **Biome from Köppen** (HY-57): replaces the hand-tuned table with Beck's published thresholds.
5. **Fertility and arable from setting, climate and alluvium** (HY-58): sourced drivers, GAEZ totals as the bound.
6. **Multi-resolution rivers and terrain** (HY-59): T2 with mean conservation and fixed river crossings.
7. **Wider river tests** (HY-60).

## Open questions

1. Is the planet grid allowed to change elevation after spec 31 (erosion changes saved fields and the golden hash), or does erosion only feed new fields?
2. Lake evaporation rate over open water: PET from RV-5 or a separate lake rule? Caspian and Great Salt Lake evaporation numbers were not read.
3. Stream power K, m and the uplift scale: read Cordonnier 2016 and Braun and Willett 2013 for the values they used.
4. Rain seasonality: is a shifted latitude profile good enough for Köppen's s/w letters, or do we need monsoon winds?
5. The combined Ganges-Brahmaputra-Meghna discharge and basin area (probably near 38,000 m³/s and 1.7 M km², unconfirmed).
6. Köppen main-class land shares on Earth: compute from Beck's GeoTIFF in the data pipeline.
7. GAEZ suitability shares by climate zone: needed to turn HY-58 from a total check into a per-class table.
8. Hexagonal child weights for HY-50: do we store explicit child area shares, or move to an aperture that nests exactly?
9. Which classification Songs of the Eons uses.
