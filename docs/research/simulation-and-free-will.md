The user shared three videos on whether reality is a simulation and whether we have free will. Our game is a deterministic simulation of a world of people, so both questions have a literal reading here: what a simulator has to do to stay consistent, and in what sense a pop computed from seed and state "chooses".

## What we take

**1. Consistency without the simulator's cheats** (FW-66, FW-67). Bostrom's simulator saves cost the way our detail levels do (FW-9) and repairs slips by editing memories or rerunning (FW-10, FW-12). We cannot repair that way: watching changes nothing (0061), and in multiplayer every observer must see one world. So every detail the player can see must be either derived from the seed and the coarse state (keyed and pure) or committed when it is first observed. Focus and compression §3 already says this; the argument shows there is no third option.

**2. Pops choose the way compatibilists say people do** (FW-68, FW-69, FW-73). The useful idea from the free-will debate is not whether pops are free (they are fully determined) but when an observer sees a choice: when the act flows from the agent's own reasons and would change with them (FW-30), and when it is explained through beliefs and wants rather than mechanism (FW-41) and shows feeling (FW-43). Laypeople grant free will to a predicted agent (FW-40) and blame tracks the choice capacity they see in the agent (FW-39). For us:
- every decision keeps its winning reason (and the runner-up), as refusals already do (pop states and actions §11);
- the inspector and the diary (0067 rule 6) say "went for fuel because the fridge was warming and she feared for the insulin", not "score 0.82";
- feelings about outcomes (relief, regret, pride) belong in the diary voice, because they are what make a pop read as someone;
- arbitrary picks may stay seeded chance; nobody asks why a pop took the left tin.

**3. The player as the outside will** (FW-70, FW-74, FW-75). In our world the players are the only causes the seed does not determine. Possession (0062) is that will entering a pop; detachment (0066) is it leaving. Two lessons follow. The Sims made autonomy worse on purpose so the player mattered (FW-50); our parity rule forbids that, so the player's value must be knowledge, plans and goals (FW-74). And the handover should be smooth both ways: on release the pop resumes from the same state (pop states and actions §11); whether it should finish the player's current intent first is an open question (FW-51).

**4. The run-ahead is Laplace's demon, with Popper's catch** (FW-71, FW-72). Running the world ahead computes the future, but only the future without new input; showing it to someone who can act falsifies it. That is why the view never shows the future, and why pops' forecasts are beliefs (spec 39), never reads of the run-ahead. Irreducibility also says where the run-ahead's cost goes: jumps are cheap where closed forms exist, and hot rules must be stepped.

**What we don't take.** Tests for whether our universe is simulated (FW-14 to FW-22) and the observer-dependent reading of quantum mechanics (FW-22) have no bearing on the design. Ethics of simulated minds (FW-63, FW-64) does not arise at our fidelity (FW-76), though diaries with feelings may make players care about pops, which is the point.

## Open questions (carried to the design docs)

- [Cameras and modes](../design/cameras-modes.md) Open: when a player releases a pop, should it finish the player's current intent before its AI decides again, and for how long (FW-51, FW-70)?
- [Focus and compression](../design/focus-and-compression.md) Open: forecasts the player sees (weather, trends, advisors) as beliefs from the forecaster's knowledge, never from the run-ahead (FW-71)?
