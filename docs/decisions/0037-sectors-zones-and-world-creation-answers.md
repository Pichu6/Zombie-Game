# 0037 · Sectors and zones; answers on forced labour, province count, map realism, uneven development, map editing

- **Date:** 2026-10-06
- **Status:** Decided by the user (answers to the open questions of [world systems](../design/world-systems.md), now section 9). Models are Proposals.
- **Extends:** [0036](0036-worlds-start-with-nations-and-everything-follows-logic.md), [0016](0016-sandbox-setup-and-layer-flow.md).

## Decisions

1. **Forced labour:** the user was not thinking of it but finds it a "cool touch of a possibility". It stays an open possibility for later, **not part of the plan**: nothing is built or scheduled for it, and it would need its own decision before any design.
2. **Granularity:** **dozens of sectors, designed to scale** to more.
3. **World realism:** worlds are realistic to **physics and math** (area, climate, geology, resources behave by their laws), not necessarily 1:1 with Earth.
4. **Uneven development at the start:** yes; nations start at different levels of development, read from what each actually holds.
5. **Map editing before the run:** yes, **much like starting a new city in Cities: Skylines**. The world is **divided into zones or sectors**, used for **nation borders** and for **placing cities and buildings**.

## Naming

- **Sector:** the control unit a nation owns and macro orders aim at (what the first draft of [world systems](../design/world-systems.md) called a province).
- **Zone:** a land-use area inside a sector where cities and buildings are placed, painted by the user or generated (Cities: Skylines zoning).
